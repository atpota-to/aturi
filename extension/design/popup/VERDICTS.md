# Panel verdicts

Two judges compared the six popup models (round 1), two critics reviewed the combined mockup (round 2), and two judges scored the three Inspect-tab ledger directions that came before. Scores are 1 to 5 per criterion.

## Round 1, judge 1 (interaction and information architecture)

Ranking: drawers, one-sheet, pulled-card, copy-menu, two-tabs-renamed, command-line

Recommendation: Build the drawers model as the spine: one stack of named drawers with faces (This page with the ledger and the band, Open in with the recommended rows in its face, Also on this page, Auto-redirect) puts all three jobs one click from the record and gives the referenced records and the global switch a legible home without a tab named for a verb. Graft one-sheet's no-data page onto it (lookup, Seen lately, group fronts with their marks, nothing to scroll) and two-tabs' one-line sticky strip that exists only while auto-redirect is on, with the pause; and watch the fold, since the ledger-first order leaves two app rows, so be ready to rest the ledger on three rows or put Open in first. If the drawer state machine is more than the owner wants to build now, ship two-tabs-renamed first: its This page tab is the same index card, it fixes the tab names and the footer today, and it converts into drawers later by deleting the tab bar.

### drawers: 37/40
clarity 5, jump 4, copy 5, hand-off 5, simplicity 4, scale 5, character 5, first 600px 4

Strengths: post-light__view.png and post-light__full.png: one stack of four drawers named for what they hold (This page, Open in, Also on this page · 5, Auto-redirect) that reads correctly with everything shut; each closed drawer keeps a useful face (the two recommended rows, the referenced authors, 'Off · links open where they are' with its switch). The ledger copies in one click with the whole row turning green and nothing moving; the ⌘C cap gives the missing keyboard path. One Open in box with paper guide strips and counts replaces six tabbed boxes. Drawers remember state, so the popup shortens to the user's habit. nopage-light__view.png: lookup field and Recent chips inside This page, the apps in a drawer the user can shut. The most literal card catalog of the six and the closest to the site's own ledger.

Weaknesses: With Open in pulled out it is as long as today (1982px in post-light__full.png) and only two app rows make the first 600; the short popup depends on remembered drawer state. A live switch inside a drawer face makes a tap on the Auto-redirect face ambiguous (open or toggle). Auto-redirect state and the pause sit at the bottom of the stack, off-screen right after a redirect. 'Scan again' and the ⌘C cap add furniture to the fold. The no-data 'Jump to an app' drawer is still 30 rows (nopage-light__full.png, 1742px). The references face lists authors, which says little about why to open it.

### command-line: 29/40
clarity 3, jump 4, copy 5, hand-off 5, simplicity 2, scale 4, character 3, first 600px 3

Strengths: post-light__view.png: the only model with a keyboard story end to end: ↵ opens the recommended app on open, ⌘↵ copies its link, ⌘C copies the AT URI, ↑ walks into the ledger. Typing filters every drawer at once (post-typed-light__view.png: 'lea' leaves Lea, Leaflet, Pinkleap, one reference and a 'Go to lea on aturi.to' row), which is the one idea here that scales to any number of apps or references. Weaknesses: At a glance it is a search box over a record, the wrong first read on a page that already has a record; the pre-selected row with key caps is a surprise to a mouse user (post-light__view.png). The 82px head plus the full ledger spends the fold: Open in starts at 468 and two app rows show. The most kinds of control of the six (field, selection, key legends, guide strips, drawer tabs, ledger, jump link, Extension rows with a switch, theme and scan). The longest resting page (2266px, post-light__full.png). Key caps and a selected-row inset bar read as a dev palette, a step outside Field Notes even though the field itself is the site's.

Worth grafting:
- one-sheet: the whole no-data page (one-line notice with Scan again, lookup field with Open, Seen lately rows, five group fronts with icon strips that open to rows; 645px, nothing to scroll) in place of drawers' 30-row Jump to an app (nopage-light__view.png, nopage-group-open-light__view.png).
- one-sheet / command-line: a moss shuffle mark beside Settings while auto-redirect is on, jumping to its row, so the global state is glanceable from the top of any page (post-redirect-on-light__view.png).
- two-tabs-renamed / pulled-card: a 36-40px sticky strip only while auto-redirect is on, 'Auto-redirect on · posts open in Anisota · Pause on this tab', and pulled-card's quiet paused state (no amber) (post-on-light__view.png, rest-on-light__view.png).
- command-line: the 'Also on this page · 5 ↓' jump on This page's tab line, so the references are announced in the first 600 without costing height; ↵ opens the recommended app and ⌘C copies the AT URI (drawers already hints ⌘C); the typed filter as a later addition for long app lists, above all on the no-data page (post-typed-light__view.png).
- copy-menu: the two-verb reading of the card (Copy, Open on aturi.to) as the wording of This page's face when the drawer is shut; the footnote line '5 more records on this page' as the shut-state face of the references drawer; and its rule that the toolbar badge counts unique records (6), not hits (7) (post-closed-light__view.png, post-footnote-light__view.png).
- pulled-card: 'other posts this page references' as the references face instead of a list of authors; the Recent drawer showing record kind and preview; the profile-page band wording 'Open on aturi.to · repo, PDS, collections' (post-light__view.png, nopage-light__view.png).
- two-tabs-renamed: Scan again on the references drawer's edge, since that list is what a rescan rebuilds, rather than on This page's tab line (post-thispage-light__view.png).
- drawers itself: keep the guide strips with counts inside one Open in box and the whole-row green copied state with nothing moving; they are the parts the other models should borrow.

Risks of the top choice:
- Height: with Open in pulled out the post page is as long as today (1982px); the short popup depends on remembered drawer state and on users shutting Open in, which then hides 28 apps behind a click for anyone whose client is not recommended. Recents in the Open in face mitigates it; so does resting the ledger on three rows.
- Fold: the ledger-first order leaves two app rows in the first 600 (post-light__view.png). If jump is the dominant job in use, this is the wrong order; test Open in first, or This page shut by default with its face showing the record line.
- A live switch inside the Auto-redirect drawer's face makes a tap ambiguous (open the drawer or flip the switch); give the switch its own hit area or move it into the body and keep the state in words on the face.
- Auto-redirect state and the pause are at the bottom of the stack, off-screen right after a redirect, the moment the pause matters most; the strip-when-on graft fixes it but brings a footer back in that one state.
- Furniture creep in the first 600: four drawer tabs, guide strips, Scan again and the ⌘C cap; show the key cap only on keyboard focus and keep Scan again off the record page.
- Drawer state must persist per page kind and reset sensibly; a remembered 'This page shut' on a new record hides the ledger, and a remembered 'Open in shut' hides the apps on a page whose recommendation is wrong.
- The no-data drawer is still a 30-row list (nopage-light__full.png, 1742px) until one-sheet's page is grafted; and the references face lists authors, which says little about why to open it.
- The model is more build than two-tabs-renamed: open/close with memory, faces that differ per drawer, guide strips, and the ⌘C handler; if that cost is the blocker, two-tabs-renamed is the honest first step.

## Round 1, judge 2 (visual design, the system)

Ranking: one-sheet, drawers, pulled-card, two-tabs-renamed, copy-menu, command-line

Recommendation: Build one-sheet: one scroll with the page's record as an index card (ledger of six identifiers, one-click copy, one 'Open on aturi.to' band) first, the waypoints under it, and the references and auto-redirect filed at the end; it is the only model whose first 600px passes the brief's test for all three jobs while staying inside Field Notes (post-light__view.png). ## Round 2, critic 1 (interaction)

What works: The inline ledger is the right answer to the judges' notes: all six identifiers with the CID back, values wrapped at slashes and dots rather than hand-cut, the rkey lifted in the primary weight so the record's own name stands out, a copied row in the accent-soft family, and it pushes the Recommended drawer down instead of covering it while the first recommended row still sits inside 600px. The folder tabs read as the card's own sheet in both themes (crop-checked: the open tab runs into the card through the hairline, filed tabs sit a step below), the pinned "This page" with its window glyph stays distinguishable when filed, and a reference card says where it was found ("In structured data") and shows a two-line preview, which is what tells two posts by the same author apart. The three jobs stay one obvious step each: jump is one click, copy is Copy then a row, hand-off is one click, a referenced record is one click on its tab. The header search is discoverable (a standard magnifier, pressed while open), the drawer reads as a grown bar with the site's 36px field and moss focus edge, it has a visible close and Esc with focus returned to the glyph, "Go to mitsu on aturi.to · Enter" spells out what Enter does, and Seen lately as suggestions with host and page count is the right reuse of the one-sheet feature. The no-data page gives one useful thing to do (the field in the card) with Seen lately as its own sheet and shorter shortcut rows, no wall. The per-page auto-redirect band on the card's bottom edge puts the pause where its scope is, the bar holds brand, switch, glyph and settings at 380 without crowding, and dark holds everywhere.

Must-fixes (all applied or answered in copy-menu-v2/NOTES.md):
- The overflowing tab strip gives no readable way to reach hidden records. The third tab is cut mid-handle under the fade ("bjkeefe.bsky.soc"), a bare "5" (and "40" on post-many) sits on the filed baseline at the strip's end and reads as a badge on that cut tab rather than "more beyond"; it is also the total, not what is hidden, so it does not tell the user anything about the strip. The scrollbar is hidden, so a mouse user has no scroll affordance at all.
