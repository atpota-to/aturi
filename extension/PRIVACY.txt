ATURI BROWSER EXTENSION - PRIVACY POLICY
Last updated: October 27, 2026

Canonical URL: https://aturi.to/extension/privacy


SUMMARY

The Aturi browser extension does not collect, transmit, or sell any personal
data. It has no analytics, no telemetry, no tracking, no advertising, and no
remote code. Everything you configure stays in your own browser.

The extension does, however, read limited information from pages you visit
so it can offer waypoints and show the AT URIs a page carries. Page reading
happens entirely inside your browser; the extension never sends page
contents to Aturi-operated servers. The extension also makes anonymous
lookups to public atproto services (Bluesky AppView, PLC directory, and the
Personal Data Servers that hold the records) when you open the popup on a
page with records, use the waypoint picker, or import your declared clients
in its settings. The details of every network request and every part of the
page that is read are described below.


1. WHAT THE EXTENSION STORES

The extension saves your preferences locally using your browser's built-in
extension storage (chrome.storage.sync, with a fallback to
chrome.storage.local if the sync quota is exceeded). The data stored is
limited to:

  - Your auto-redirect preferences (whether it's on, your favorite waypoint
    per content family, and per-source overrides).
  - Custom waypoints you define (name, domain, URL templates, and category).
  - Your waypoint groups, ordering, and visibility choices.
  - A capped local list of recently used waypoints (up to 20 entries) used
    to surface frequently used destinations in the popup. You can disable
    this in the History tab and clear it at any time.
  - UI preferences such as compact mode and open-in-new-tab.

No part of this data is sent to Aturi, the developer, or any third party.
If you are signed into your browser and have extension sync enabled, your
browser vendor (Google, Mozilla, Apple, etc.) may sync this data across
your devices through their own infrastructure under their own privacy
policy. Aturi has no access to that synced data.

Identity records and record previews fetched for the popup's record card
live only in popup memory while the popup is open and are discarded when
you close it. One exception: when history is enabled (the default), the
extension keeps a "Seen lately" list of the accounts whose records it saw
on pages where you opened the popup, so the popup can suggest them when you
look something up. Each entry holds the account's DID, handle and PDS host,
how many pages it turned up on, and a one-way hash of the last page counted
(never the page's address). The list is capped at 50 accounts, stored only
in this browser's local extension storage (it is not synced), gated on the
same toggle as the recents list, and removed when you clear or disable
history.


2. WHAT THE EXTENSION READS FROM PAGES

The extension includes two content scripts that run on ordinary web pages.
Both run entirely inside your browser. No data they read is ever sent to
Aturi-operated servers; the extension only uses what they read to render
the popup locally.

  - Passive head detection (detect-head.content.ts). On every page load, a
    tiny script runs once and looks for <link href="at://..."> elements in
    the document head. It does not read any other part of the page, does
    not modify the page, and does not send anything anywhere until you
    open the toolbar popup. If the popup asks (and only then), it returns
    the AT URI it found so the popup can show relevant waypoints. This is
    how the extension supports apps like Leaflet, Offprint, and pckt,
    which advertise their AT URI in the head rather than the URL.

  - Page scan (inspect-scan.content.ts). This script scans the page for
    AT URIs (at://...) in the document head, OpenGraph and Twitter meta
    tags, anchor hrefs, JSON-LD blocks, and a capped portion (up to about
    2 MB) of the page's visible text. It runs in two modes:

      * Passively, when the "highlight Atmosphere pages" badge is enabled
        (the default): on each page load and SPA route change it runs the
        scan and reports only the *count* of AT URIs it found to the
        extension's own background worker, which drives the toolbar badge
        and icon. No page content leaves the page, only a number. You can
        turn this off with the passive-scan toggle in the options page,
        after which the script stays idle until you ask for a scan.

      * On demand, when you open the popup: it runs the same scan and
        returns the URIs it found, deduplicated, along with a short
        surrounding text snippet for context, so the popup can show each
        record the page carries as a tab on its record card.

    In both modes the scan stays inside your browser and never transmits
    page content to Aturi-operated servers.

Neither script injects content into pages you visit, modifies the DOM,
intercepts form input, executes remote code, or participates in any
cross-origin tracking. You can disable them entirely by uninstalling the
extension or, on most browsers, by revoking host permissions for specific
sites in the extension settings page.


3. NETWORK REQUESTS

The extension makes a small number of well-defined network requests, each
tied to a specific user action. None of these requests go to Aturi-operated
servers; they go directly from your browser to the relevant public atproto
service, whose own privacy policy applies.

  - Handle resolution. When the popup needs to turn a handle (e.g.
    example.bsky.social) into a DID -- for example, because a waypoint you
    clicked requires a DID -- the extension calls
    https://public.api.bsky.app/xrpc/com.atproto.identity.resolveHandle.
    The handle is sent as a query parameter; nothing else is sent.

  - Record card: identity resolution. When you open the popup on a page
    with AT URIs, the extension resolves the repo identifier of the page's
    own record, and of each other record on the page, through the same
    Bluesky AppView. For did:plc:... identifiers it may fetch the DID
    document from the PLC directory (plc.directory), and for did:web:...
    identifiers it fetches the DID document from the /.well-known/did.json
    endpoint of the host named in the DID, to discover the account's handle
    (which labels the record's tab) and its PDS host.

  - Record card: repository description. For the record whose tab is open,
    the extension calls com.atproto.repo.describeRepo on the resolved PDS
    host, which returns the account's handle and collections.

  - Record card: record fetch. Once the PDS host is known, the extension
    calls com.atproto.repo.getRecord on that PDS to fetch the open record
    itself, for its CID (one of the identifiers the Copy list offers) and,
    for a record the page references, a short preview.

  - Preferred clients import. When you press Look up under "Import your
    declared clients" in the extension's settings, the extension resolves
    the handle or DID you entered (through the same AppView and DID
    document lookups as above), calls com.atproto.repo.describeRepo on that
    account's PDS, and calls com.atproto.repo.getRecord there for its
    to.aturi.client.declaration record. Nothing is fetched until you
    press the button, and nothing from the record is kept beyond the
    favorites you choose to set.

Auto-redirects do not make network requests. They are implemented with
chrome.declarativeNetRequest, which lets the browser rewrite URLs locally
based on static rules the extension generates from your preferences. The
extension does not see, read, or log the URLs you visit while redirects
run.

The extension never contacts aturi.to or any other Aturi-operated server
during normal use.


4. PERMISSIONS AND WHY THEY'RE NEEDED

  - storage - to persist your preferences, custom waypoints, and recents
    list.
  - tabs - to read the URL and the tab id of the current tab when you
    open the popup, so the extension can offer waypoints that match the
    page you're on and route the page scan request to the right tab.
  - declarativeNetRequest - to perform local URL redirects without
    exposing your browsing history to the extension.
  - clipboardWrite - to copy universal Aturi links to your clipboard when
    you use the "copy link" action.
  - host_permissions: <all_urls> - required so the popup can recognize
    Atmosphere pages on any domain (Bluesky, Leaflet, Blacksky, PDSls,
    custom waypoints you define, etc.), so declarativeNetRequest rules
    can match those domains, and so the two content scripts described
    above can run on the active tab when needed. The scripts only read
    the limited page surfaces listed in Section 2 and never transmit
    page contents off-device.


5. WHAT THE EXTENSION DOES NOT DO

  - It does not collect or transmit personal information to Aturi.
  - It does not use cookies or any tracking technology.
  - It does not run analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or A/B testing.
  - It does not load or execute remotely hosted code. All executable code
    is bundled in the released extension package and reviewed by the
    browser store.
  - It does not modify the contents of pages you visit, inject
    user-facing UI into pages, intercept form input, or read page content
    beyond the specific surfaces described in Section 2.
  - It does not show ads or include any advertising SDKs.
  - It does not sell, rent, share, or trade any data with third parties.


6. THIRD-PARTY DESTINATIONS AND SERVICES

When you click a waypoint or follow an auto-redirected link, your browser
navigates to a third-party Atmosphere client (Bluesky, Blacksky, Leaflet,
Tangled, Margin, Grain, PDSls, atp.tools, etc.) or a custom waypoint you
defined. When the popup shows a page's records, your browser makes the
lookups described in Section 3 directly to the Bluesky AppView, the PLC
directory, and the relevant Personal Data Server. Each of these
services has its own privacy policy and terms of service, and Aturi has no
control over them.


7. CHILDREN'S PRIVACY

The extension is a general-purpose link-routing and inspection utility and
is not directed at children. Because it does not collect any personal
information, it does not knowingly collect data from children under 13.


8. DATA RETENTION AND DELETION

Settings persist in your browser's extension storage for as long as the
extension is installed. You can:

  - Clear the Recents and Seen lately lists at any time from the popup
    section of the settings page.
  - Disable history tracking entirely from the same section, which also
    removes the Seen lately list.
  - Remove all extension data by uninstalling the extension. Your browser
    may also remove any synced copy via its own sync settings.
